Speaking up: phonetics in everyday practice

How short, frequent speaking moments build the confidence to address a room.

Nusrat JahanEnglish & Phonetics
27 Jul 2026
Back to the Journal27 Jul 2026  ·  6 min read

Confidence in front of a room is not a personality trait. It is a habit, and like any habit it is built in small, unremarkable repetitions.

We teach phonetics every day, in short bursts, from the earliest class. The aim is straightforward: clear sounds, a steady voice, and the willingness to speak even when the words are not perfect yet.

Why phonetics comes first

Pronunciation sits underneath every other English skill. A child who is unsure how a word sounds avoids using it, and a child who avoids words stops practising. Fix the sound and you unlock the vocabulary almost by accident.

  • Sound work happens in every class, not in a weekly special.
  • Children hear a model, copy it, then use it in a sentence.
  • Correction is quick, kind and specific: one thing at a time.
  • Every child speaks aloud every day, even for two sentences.

The goal is not a perfect accent. The goal is a child who is not afraid of their own voice.

Nusrat Jahan, English & Phonetics

From two sentences to a stage

The progression is gradual on purpose. A child might start by saying their news to the person sitting beside them. Later it is a sentence to the whole class, then a short prepared talk, then standing in front of families at our Young Speakers Showcase.

The 30-second rule

Every speaking task in the early years fits inside thirty seconds. Short tasks get finished and repeated often. Long tasks get dreaded.

At home: worth more than you think

Ask your child to tell you one thing that happened, out loud, without interruption. Then ask one follow-up question. That is it. Two minutes of undivided attention does more for spoken confidence than an hour of correction.

And when they mix languages, let it pass. Switching between Bangla and English is a sign of fluency in both, not confusion in either. The confidence comes first; the polish comes with time.
