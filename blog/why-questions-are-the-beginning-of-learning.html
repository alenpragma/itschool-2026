Why questions are the beginning of learning

A classroom where curiosity is welcomed produces learners who keep asking long after school.

Topajjol Hossain RaselHead Teacher, Luxmichari
16 Aug 2026
Back to the Journal16 Aug 2026  ·  4 min read

A child asks roughly a hundred questions a day at four years old. Somewhere between then and secondary school, most of them stop. We think that is the single most avoidable loss in education.

Walk into one of our classrooms and you will hear questions from both directions. Children ask the teacher, and the teacher asks the class something they genuinely do not know the answer to. That second habit is the harder one to build, and it changes everything.

The fear of being wrong

By the time children reach Class 3, many have learned that a question is a risk. There is a right answer, the teacher has it, and guessing wrong has a cost. Once that belief sets in, hands go down and the classroom goes quiet in the worst possible way.

  • Praise the reasoning, even when the answer misses.
  • Ask 'how did you get there?' before 'is that right?'
  • Let a wrong answer sit and be examined instead of corrected instantly.
  • Answer honestly when you do not know, then find out together.

A child who has never been allowed to be wrong in front of others will never be brave enough to think out loud.

Topajjol Hossain Rasel, Head Teacher

Questions as assessment

We learn more from a child's question than from their test score. 'Why does the moon follow the car?' tells you that a child is watching, wondering and willing to say so. That is the raw material every teacher wants.

A small habit worth copying

Keep a running 'question wall' in the classroom. Anything unanswered goes up there and gets visited on a Friday. It tells children that a good question outlives the lesson it was asked in.

What parents can do at home

When your child asks something you cannot answer, say so, plainly and comfortably. Then look it up together. The message is not that adults know everything. The message is that not knowing is a normal, ordinary, completely fine place to start.
